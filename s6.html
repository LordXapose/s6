4 · Cost-aware choice

The confusion is concentrated where it hurts: the model never over-calls an exploit (class 2 precision is a perfect 1.000), but it under-calls one — 5 of 13 real exploits get filed as benign or recon, dragging recall to 0.615. In an intrusion setting that is exactly the wrong direction: a missed exploit (false negative on class 2) is the most expensive error, far costlier than a benign sample flagged for review.

This is why bare accuracy — a comfortable 0.900 — is misleading: the two large classes (95% of the data) dominate it, so the headline can stay high while the one class you most need to catch quietly leaks. Per-class recall on class 2 is the metric that actually drives the decision.

For the wrapper choice, OvO and OvR are statistically tied here (0.865 vs 0.863), so accuracy alone does not pick a winner. I would keep the default OvO SVC — identical accuracy at this k, and OvO trains on balanced binary sub-problems rather than pitting the tiny exploit class against everything else at once, which is gentler on the minority recall that matters most. To lift class-2 recall further I would tune class_weight="balanced" or shift its decision threshold, accepting a little class-2 precision in exchange for catching more real exploits.
